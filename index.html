The problem with “just using ChatGPT”

Your staff probably already want to use AI. The question is where your clients' information goes when they do.

Public AI websites

  • Documents are sent over the internet to another company's servers.
  • You depend on that company's settings, policies, and staff.
  • Hard to answer when a client or regulator asks “where did my data go?”
  • Monthly per-person subscriptions that add up as you grow.

Private AI in your office

  • Documents stay on equipment inside your office.
  • You decide who can use it, and every use can be logged.
  • A simple answer for clients: “it never left our office.”
  • A fixed monthly support cost for the whole team.

Questions we hear

Does my data ever leave my office?

With the in-office option, no. The AI runs on equipment inside your office and is set up so it isn't reachable from the internet. Updates are applied on a schedule you approve. With the hosted pilot, data travels over an encrypted connection to dedicated equipment we operate in Central Florida — not a public cloud — and we'll explain exactly where it sits.

Is it HIPAA-compliant? What about attorney–client confidentiality?

No product makes you compliant by itself — compliance depends on how it's set up and used. Private AI makes it much simpler, because confidential information stays under your control. For medical and dental practices we can sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) where required, and every client gets a written confidentiality agreement. We'll set up access controls and logs, and we're happy to work with your compliance advisor, bar guidance, or IT provider.

Is it as smart as ChatGPT?

For everyday office work — summaries, drafts, organizing information, answering questions from your own files — today's openly available AI is very capable. We'll show you on your own kind of documents during the assessment, so you can judge for yourself before you commit.

What does it cost compared to AI subscriptions?

You pay a one-time setup, the equipment (which you own), and a fixed monthly support fee for the whole office. There are no per-person AI subscriptions and no usage meters. Whether it saves you money depends on your team size and how much you use it — we'll walk through the numbers with you honestly.

What happens if the internet goes down?

The in-office AI keeps working on your local network. You only need the internet for remote support and scheduled updates.

We already have an IT company. Do we replace them?

No. We work alongside your existing IT provider and follow their standards. They keep doing what they do; we handle the AI.

How reliable is the hosted pilot?

It's designed for pilots and lighter workloads and runs on dedicated local equipment with monitoring and a maintenance plan. It does not carry the guarantees of a large data center today. If AI becomes critical to your daily operations, we recommend the in-office option.

For your IT team

Technical details for IT providers and security reviewers.

Hardware options
StarterNVIDIA DGX Spark–class compact system (GB10, 128 GB unified memory) or equivalent OEM GB10 unit.
PracticeWorkstation with NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB), or multiple linked GB10 units.
EnterpriseNVIDIA DGX Station (GB300) class system for larger user counts; requires a dedicated circuit and cooling review (roughly 1–1.2 kW).
Models and software

Open-weight large language models (for example Qwen and GLM families) served with open-source inference engines on-device; local embeddings for retrieval over firm documents; a browser-based chat interface with SSO/local accounts. No data is sent to model vendors. Model choice is matched to the hardware and workload and benchmarked before go-live.

Network and security
  • Dedicated VLAN/segment for the AI system; firewall rules allow only internal clients.
  • No inbound ports exposed to the internet; remote support over an authenticated VPN/overlay you can disable.
  • Disk encryption, role-based access, audit logging of usage, and monitoring/alerting.
  • Change windows and updates coordinated with your IT provider; documentation handed over at go-live.
